What this test contains

25 questions on Unit 1 (Lexical Analysis), Set 1 of 2. All four subtopics are covered: tokens, lexemes and patterns; regular expressions to lexers; the structure of a Lex/Flex specification; and input buffering with lookahead. Composition: 15 MCQ, 5 MSQ, 5 NAT; difficulty 10 easy, 10 medium, 5 hard; 60 minutes, 35 marks. MCQ carries 1 mark, MSQ and NAT carry 2 marks, there is no negative marking, and an MSQ scores only when the exact set of correct options is selected. This is a practice marking scheme, not the official GATE scheme.

Concept map used to build the questions

  • Tokens, lexemes and patterns. The three-level description of a token (pattern, lexeme, token class); the maximal-munch rule and rule priority as the only tie-breaker; why a keyword rule listed after an identifier rule is dead code; symbol-table entries for identifiers but not keywords; and the division of labour between the scanner and the parser.
  • Regular expressions to lexers. Patterns are regular expressions, so the scanner is a finite automaton; regular expression → NFA → DFA → table-driven scan; the state a DFA must remember for a "last k symbols" property; the subset-construction worst case against the minimal DFA; and the boundary of the technique — counting languages such as aⁿbⁿ are not regular, so no lexer can match balanced parentheses.
  • Lex input structure. The three sections and their two %% separators; what belongs in declarations, rules and user subroutines; yytext and yyleng; longest-match-then-first-rule selection with competing numeric patterns such as INT, FLOAT and DOT; and why the automaton is compiled once, at scanner-generation time.
  • Input buffering and lookahead. The two half-buffers and the sentinel that halves the per-character work; what happens when a lexeme crosses a half boundary and why the lexeme must be moved before a refill; the bound a buffer places on lexeme length; and the single character of lookahead a DFA scanner needs, including the push-back of the character that ended the token.
