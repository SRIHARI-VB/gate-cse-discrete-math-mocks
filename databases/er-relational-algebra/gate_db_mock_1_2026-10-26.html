What this test contains

25 questions on Unit 1 (ER Models & Relational Algebra), Set 1 of 2. All four subtopics are covered: ER diagrams, cardinality and weak entities; the relational model and its constraints; relational algebra (selection, projection, join, division); and tuple and domain relational calculus. Composition: 15 MCQ, 5 MSQ, 5 NAT; difficulty 10 easy, 10 medium, 5 hard; 60 minutes, 35 marks. MCQ carries 1 mark, MSQ and NAT carry 2 marks, there is no negative marking, and an MSQ scores only when the exact set of correct options is selected. This is a practice marking scheme, not the official GATE scheme.

Concept map used to build the questions

  • ER diagrams, cardinality and weak entities. The notation set (double rectangle for a weak entity set, double diamond for its identifying relationship, double ellipse for a multivalued attribute, dashed ellipse for a derived one); reading a cardinality ratio together with a participation constraint — at most one from the ratio, at least one from total participation — and the product that bounds a weak entity set's size; aggregation as “relationship-as-entity” as opposed to generalisation and composite attributes.
  • Relational model and constraints. Entity integrity (no NULL inside a primary key) against referential integrity (a foreign key is NULL or matches a stored value), and why the two rules can bind different attributes of the same relation; superkey versus candidate key; closure computation to enumerate candidate keys, including the cyclic-dependency case where several attributes are keys on their own; counting superkeys as supersets of a candidate key.
  • Relational algebra. σ cuts rows and π cuts columns, with duplicate elimination implicit because every result is a set; union-compatibility and the derived operators (R ∩ S = R − (R − S)); natural-join size as Σc r_c·s_c and the concentration argument that maximises it; division as the “for all” operator, both by definition and through R ÷ S = πA(R) − πA((πA(R) × S) − R); and the difference-over-join pattern that turns an existential join into a universal statement.
  • Tuple and domain relational calculus. Free variables as the output columns and bound variables as projected away; membership clause → relation, attribute conditions → σ, t[list] → π; safety as confinement to the active domain, and why { t | ¬(t ∈ R) } is rejected while negated conditions inside a bounded range are fine; Codd's equivalence of algebra and safe calculus, and the one query class that escapes both — transitive closure.
  • Connections. Closure and candidate-key counting return in Unit 3 (normalisation); division and the “for all” pattern underpin SQL's nested NOT EXISTS queries in Unit 2; join-size arithmetic and selectivity recur in Unit 5 (indexing and cost estimation).
