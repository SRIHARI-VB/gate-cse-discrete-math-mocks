What this test contains

25 questions on Unit 1 (Number Systems & Representation), Set 1 of 2. All four subtopics are covered: conversion between binary, octal, hexadecimal and decimal including fractional numerals; one's and two's complement representation and negation; the IEEE 754 single and double formats with normal and subnormal numbers; and the ranges of signed and unsigned fields with the rules for detecting overflow. Composition: 15 MCQ, 5 MSQ, 5 NAT; difficulty 10 easy, 10 medium, 5 hard; 60 minutes, 35 marks. MCQ carries 1 mark, MSQ and NAT carry 2 marks, there is no negative marking, and an MSQ scores only when the exact set of correct options is selected. This is a practice marking scheme, not the official GATE scheme.

Concept map used to build the questions

  • Base conversions. The group widths that make conversion mechanical (3 bits per octal digit, 4 per hexadecimal digit); place-value expansion for the general base; fractional bits as halves, quarters and eighths; solving for an unknown base from an equation in the digits; and the habit of checking a conversion through decimal.
  • One's and two's complement. Negation as invert-then-add-one; reading a pattern as unsigned minus 2n; the end-around carry that one's complement needs and two's complement discards; sign extension; and the single value at −2n−1 that is its own negation.
  • Floating point and IEEE 754. The 1 + 8 + 23 and 1 + 11 + 52 field splits; the excess-127 and excess-1023 biases; decoding (−1)s × 1.f × 2E−bias; the implicit leading 1; subnormals with an all-zero exponent; and the reserved patterns for zero, infinity and NaN.
  • Range and overflow. The intervals 0..2n−1 and −2n−1..2n−1−1; modular wraparound by 2n; the sign-based overflow rule and its two-carry equivalent; and why the unsigned carry flag is not the signed overflow flag.
