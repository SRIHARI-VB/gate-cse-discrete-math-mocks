What this test contains

25 questions on Unit 1 (Regular Languages & Finite Automata), Set 1 of 2. All four subtopics are covered: DFA construction and minimisation, NFA to DFA by subset construction, regular expressions, and the pumping lemma for regular languages. Composition: 15 MCQ, 5 MSQ, 5 NAT; difficulty 10 easy, 10 medium, 5 hard; 60 minutes, 35 marks. MCQ carries 1 mark, MSQ and NAT carry 2 marks, there is no negative marking, and an MSQ scores only when the exact set of correct options is selected. This is a practice marking scheme, not the official GATE scheme.

Concept map used to build the questions

  • DFA construction & minimisation. What a finite automaton can remember and what it cannot; parity and modular-count languages and their state counts; the product construction for conjoined conditions; reachability pruning followed by table filling; the meaning of state equivalence and why the minimal DFA is unique.
  • NFA to DFA: subset construction. Existential acceptance; the 2ⁿ subset bound and why it is only a bound; ε-closures as the start state and as the successor operation; determinisation followed by minimisation, and the classical 2ⁿ-state language that forces every subset to appear.
  • Regular expressions. Reading an expression as a machine (loop = even contribution, trailing factor = odd contribution); anchoring versus substring forms such as (a+b)*abb against (a+b)*abb(a+b)*; identities settled by a separating string; counting the strings of a fixed length by complementing the language.
  • Pumping lemma for regular languages. The lemma as a necessary condition only; choosing the witness string so that every legal split fails; pumping up versus pumping down depending on whether the language is one-sided; Myhill–Nerode classes as the sharp test, and the family of non-regular languages that recur in GATE papers.
